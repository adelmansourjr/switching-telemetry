# Frozen protocol for telemetry with one unknown faulty counter

## Question and claims

Consider the same two-relay finite-window drift identity and occupancy simplex used in the original telemetry study. The two mean-displacement readings remain within their calibrated error bounds. Before seeing any reading, choose three distinct scalar counters from the six-counter menu. At most one chosen counter may report an arbitrary value. Counter data are read once per window, and the estimator is not told which report is faulty. The exact claim concerns this bounded-fault observation model on the occupancy simplex. Simulation checks whether the resulting additional consistency constraints matter at realistic finite-window resolutions.

The primary test isolates decoding, with the same three counters and transcript for both methods. Compare the sharp interval from the union of two-counter feasible sets against the robust median of the lower endpoints and of the upper endpoints of the three single-counter intervals. These medians are sound because any two honest intervals contain the truth. For an empty single interval, use lower endpoint plus infinity and upper endpoint minus infinity before taking medians. The union decoder keeps the common geometry of the two counters that must be simultaneously satisfiable. Both receive the same displacement readings.

Choose the proposed triplet before observing readings by minimising the exact worst-case diameter. With three counters and one fault, that diameter is the maximum of the three single-counter diameters with sound displacement included. This gives the same optimal score as explicitly evaluating all triplets. Allow ties at 11 decimal places and select the first menu-ordered triplet. Compare precision-only selection, the three largest null-space projections divided by error radius, and the best fixed distinct triplet selected by realised paired robust width on 32 development fields. Every triplet comparator uses the same union decoder. The original all-three consistency estimator is an additional unprotected control with abstention when its set is empty. A fault-location oracle that omits the damaged counter and knows the other two are sound measures the cost of not knowing the location.

## Splits and perturbations

Draw 32 development, 96 primary and 48 shift fields from independent streams 920159, 920173 and 920189. Exclude exact uncoupled fields and the three published fields. The primary and development rate alphabets are integers 1 to 7. The shift alphabet is {1, 2, 4, 8, 16}, fields are disjoint across splits, and the shift uses delayed sign kernels and threefold displacement error. Each field has two independent settings using the original project's initial state, step, horizon, relay-width and sensor-radius laws, with independent measurement seeds 124203, 124211 and 124223. Nominal and development kernels are sign, hysteresis and smooth tanh. The shift uses delay, hysteresis and smooth tanh. Use random lag from one to three steps when a delay kernel is present. Random draws keep the seed order fixed.

First construct a shared honest tape. For each chosen counter in turn, compare four prescribed single-fault reports: subtract four error radii, add four error radii, stick at 1/2, and substitute the predicted counter value farthest from the honest value among the occupancy vertices consistent with displacement alone. The first two reports are clipped to [0,1]. The fourth report is feasible when that counter is considered alone. These interventions are deterministic after the honest tape. For each trajectory and triplet, report the maximum width across its 12 fault probes and the average across probes. Report clean width separately. Paired estimators and selectors share trajectory settings and underlying honest readings. Each selector faces the same probe rule on its own three chosen counters. The protocol assumes three distinct predicates at comparable scalar-read cost and faults limited to one channel. This is not a sensor-calibration or clinical experiment.

## Evaluation

Primary endpoint is the test-field-averaged difference in worst-probe interval width between median and union decoding on the minimax triplet. Require no excluded truths or incorrect strict-threshold decisions by either fault-tolerant decoder, at least 0.005 absolute and 10% relative improvement, and a paired 95% bootstrap interval with positive lower endpoint. Resample whole fields 4,000 times with NumPy seed 308247, keeping all their trajectories and probes together. The practical gate is for this decoder comparison. Triplet-selection comparisons and shift tests are secondary. Mark resolution only when the full interval is strictly beyond one of the original thresholds .15, .25, .35, .45. Report coverage, empty sets and incorrect calls for the all-three control. Unsafe control width is conditional on being nonempty and is never compared as a success measure.

Verify 100% coverage for every triplet and every single-fault nominal or shifted tape, verify sample widths do not exceed the appropriate diameter, and construct a pair of dissimilar occupancies that attain the robust diameter by sharing one possible transcript for every minimax configuration. Keep a separate rational fixture that exhausts a fixed finite grid, including the two occupancies and a common transcript that show why the single shared honest counter is the only guaranteed overlap when failure location is unknown.
