# Mike Jeffrey reading notes

The University of Bristol's hidden dynamics project asks how interacting discontinuous decisions produce uncertainty even when each decision rule seems straightforward, and whether this persists in larger networks. Mike Jeffrey describes his broader research as geometrical asymptotics, with discontinuities and singularities as recurring examples. This project addresses the information problem raised by the two-switch case. It asks what measurements would recover a particular finite-window decision statistic. [Bristol project description](https://www.bristol.ac.uk/research/groups/engineering-mathematics/phd-opportunities/#hidden-dynamics-of-decision-making) · [Bristol profile](https://www.bristol.ac.uk/people/person/Mike-Jeffrey-e10badd6-6606-4eba-a664-83f927869db4/).

- **Catsis, Hall and Jeffrey (2025), Perturbation and determinacy of nonsmooth systems.** Sections 3.3 and 4 distinguish the weighted occupancy sums constrained by sliding from the individual occupancies that can vary strongly with regularisation. In the affine case the identifiable sums reduce to the two marginal switch duties. Figure 5 makes the practical issue vivid by connecting peaks in the joint occupancy to changes in the attractor near the switch intersection. Appendix A explores several regularisations of the same ideal system. The pilot extends the drift identity to finite measured windows, adds a certificate for a single candidate counter, and exhibits reachable histories whose individual duties coincide. [Final article](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.physd.2025.134679) · [Author manuscript](https://nonsmooth.land/wp-content/uploads/2025/05/2024_paradigm.pdf).
- **Catsis, Hall and Jeffrey (2024), The hidden sensitivity of non-smooth dynamics.** Sections 4 and 7.2 distinguish transition counts from time spent in a mode. In the delayed one-switch example, transition counts expose parameter sensitivity that can disappear from occupancy alone. The two-switch example shows that the sensitivity can then alter large-scale behaviour. This opens a specific next question. Can an instrument that reports transitions rather than dwell time recover the same information, and what timestamp resolution would that require? The answer may depend on whether the sensor retains the phase relationship between the switches. [Article](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.physd.2024.134165) · [Author manuscript](https://nonsmooth.land/wp-content/uploads/2024/01/2023_1dpilotmap.pdf).
- **Jeffrey and Pereira (2026), Bifurcations and catastrophes of dynamical systems with centre dimension one.** The paper uses underlying catastrophes to identify contact-equivalent families and, in the corank-one, centre-dimension-one case, topological bifurcation classes. It covers vector fields and maps. The methodological link is the value of identifying the structure that survives model reduction. Its bifurcation theorem concerns a distinct setting from the relay occupancy experiment. [Published paper](https://doi.org/10.1088/1361-6544/ae3b90).
- **Pereira, Jeffrey and Novaes (2026), Averaging theory and catastrophes.** For periodically perturbed systems, the paper studies when non-hyperbolic behaviour of the averaged guiding system persists in the original dynamics. Its sufficient conditions use unfolding theory and distinguish universal bifurcations from nonversal cases. This suggests a useful distinction for a meeting between what survives time averaging, what survives a switch regularisation, and what must be measured directly. [Journal article](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0022039626000331) · [Author preprint](https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.11054).
- **Jeffrey (2026), Underlying catastrophes.** The article develops solvable conditions to locate collisions of stationary points and illustrates phenomena including simultaneous saddle-node and saddle-focus bifurcations. It is relevant to the second Bristol project on singularities and pattern formation. That is a distinct direction from the present information experiment. [Published paper](https://doi.org/10.1007/s44425-026-00046-7).

A focused question for a supervisor conversation is whether the counter measurements should be selected to resolve whole joint occupancies or only the weighted combinations relevant to a decision. In larger networks the available readout rows may themselves come from local counters. Extending the difference-set certificate to that case gives an identifiable mathematical target. A physical bench would test the additional assumption that the error bounds survive changes in the hidden switching rule.
